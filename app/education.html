Literacy and learning

Education

Adult literacy reached 98.6% in 2025 and youth literacy 99.5%. Net secondary enrolment rose to 88.4%, while gross tertiary enrolment passed two in five young adults, at 41.2%.

Enrolment

Secondary schooling closes the gap

Net primary enrolment rose from 93.1% to 96.8% between 2016 and 2025. The larger gain came at secondary level, where the net rate rose from 79.8% to 88.4%, helped by a school-building programme and cash transfers for low-income households.

Gross tertiary enrolment rose from 31.5% to 41.2%. Most of the increase is in three-year professional and technical programmes rather than academic degrees.

Resources

More teachers, longer schooling

The pupil–teacher ratio in primary schools fell from 17.6 to 14.2 pupils per teacher. Public spending on education rose from 4.4% to 4.9% of GDP, or about $33.7 billion at 2025 prices.

Adults aged 25 and over now have 10.8 years of schooling on average, up from 9.7 in 2016. Youth literacy is 0.9 percentage points above the adult rate, reflecting the expansion of upper secondary education since the 1990s.
