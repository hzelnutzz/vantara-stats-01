The 2025 labour market

Recovery is broad but youth lag

Unemployment fell for a fourth consecutive year, from 9.4% in 2020 to 6.2% in 2025. Employment reached 13.49 million, 190,000 more than in 2024. Participation edged up to 63.4% of the working-age population, close to its pre-pandemic level.

Youth unemployment fell to 14.6% from a peak of 21.8% in 2020, but it remains 2.4 times the rate for all workers. Long-term unemployment, defined as job search lasting 12 months or more, accounted for 31% of total unemployment, down from 38% in 2021.

About the survey

How unemployment is measured

The Labour Force Survey interviews about 4,800 households every quarter across the six statistical regions. Unemployment follows the International Labour Organization definition: people without work, available to start within two weeks, and actively seeking work in the previous four weeks.

Annual figures are averages of the four quarterly estimates and are not seasonally adjusted. The survey covers people aged 15 and over living in private households; people in institutions and collective dwellings are excluded.
